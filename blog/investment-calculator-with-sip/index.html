Financial Calculators

How to Use These Financial Calculators

Use the calculator as an educational planning tool rather than a prediction of future returns. Enter assumptions for contribution, time period and expected rate, then compare multiple scenarios instead of relying on one optimistic number. Small changes in return or time can materially change the final result because compounding accumulates over many periods.

Real investments may include taxes, fees, inflation, market volatility and irregular contributions, so the displayed result should be treated as an estimate. A useful planning exercise is to compare conservative, moderate and optimistic assumptions and focus on the contribution level you can sustain consistently.

If you are comparing SIP or lump-sum scenarios, keep the time horizon and rate assumptions consistent so the comparison stays meaningful. The calculator is most useful for exploring how contribution discipline and time affect an estimate, not for selecting a specific investment product.
