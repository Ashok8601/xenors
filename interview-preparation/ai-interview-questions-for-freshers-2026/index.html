Core interview questions and concise model answers

1. What is artificial intelligence?

AI is the broad field of building systems that perform tasks requiring perception, reasoning, language understanding, prediction or decision-making. In an interview, separate AI from machine learning: ML is one way to build AI systems by learning patterns from data.

2. What is the difference between AI, ML and deep learning?

AI is the umbrella goal, machine learning learns statistical patterns from data, and deep learning is a subset of ML based on multi-layer neural networks. Use a concrete example such as spam detection versus an image classifier to show the difference.

3. What is supervised learning?

Supervised learning uses labelled examples to learn a mapping from inputs to targets. Mention classification versus regression, a validation set, and a metric tied to the cost of mistakes.

4. What is overfitting?

Overfitting happens when a model learns training-specific noise instead of general patterns. Diagnose it by a widening train-versus-validation gap and mitigate it with simpler models, regularisation, more representative data or better validation.

5. How do you choose an evaluation metric?

Start from the business cost of errors. Accuracy may be poor for imbalanced classes; precision matters when false positives are costly, recall matters when misses are costly, and ranking systems may need MAP or NDCG.

6. What is data leakage?

Leakage occurs when training data contains information that would not be available at prediction time. Common examples are target-derived features, future timestamps and preprocessing performed before the train-test split.

7. How would you describe an AI project in an interview?

Use a compact story: problem, data, baseline, approach, evaluation, deployment constraints, failure you found, and what changed because of your work. Be explicit about your own contribution.

8. What would you do if a model is accurate offline but poor in production?

Check feature parity, data drift, label delay, serving bugs, threshold differences and whether the offline metric matches the user outcome. Compare production slices against the validation distribution before retraining blindly.

Revision checklist

  • Name one failure mode and one measurement.
  • State when you would choose a simpler alternative.
  • Separate what you implemented personally from team work.
