Gemini 4 Carbon: Features, Price, Benchmarks & Carbon vs Argon

Google made Gemini 4 Carbon broadly available on November 10, 2026. Carbon is positioned as Google's strongest frontier model for difficult long-horizon work: multi-step software engineering, cybersecurity defense, scientific reasoning, dense document analysis and agentic workflows that may run for extended periods.

Gemini 4 Carbon in 30 seconds

Gemini 4 Carbon is the high-reasoning model in Google's Gemini 4 family. Google says Argon remains the better default for most software engineering and knowledge-work tasks, while Carbon is intended for problems that need deeper exploration, extended verification loops and stronger long-horizon reasoning.

Carbon is available to paying developers through the Gemini API in Google AI Studio, to enterprises through Gemini for business, and is rolling out to Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app, Google Antigravity and AI Mode in Search. Google's current introductory API price is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens; Google says the price rises after November 30, 2026 to $4 input / $20 output per million tokens.

What is Gemini 4 Carbon?

Gemini 4 Carbon is Google's newest frontier reasoning model and the higher-compute sibling to Gemini 4 Argon. It was first announced on October 28, 2026 with a deliberately limited cyber-defense rollout, then Google expanded availability on November 10 to developers, businesses, scientists and consumers through selected products and plans.

The model is designed around a specific class of problem: work where a model has to stay on task across many steps, inspect large amounts of evidence, recover from failed approaches and verify its own output before finishing. That is different from simply answering a difficult question in one response.

Google highlights use cases including cross-system code migrations, complex debugging, full-stack application development, cybersecurity vulnerability analysis, financial and legal research, scientific data analysis, multimodal file understanding and multi-agent coordination.

Google's launch announcement includes an important deadline: after the introductory period ends on November 30, 2026, the listed public-access Carbon price becomes $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens.

That makes model selection important. If Carbon uses more reasoning tokens to solve a problem that Argon can already solve reliably, using Carbon for every request can be wasteful. Carbon makes the most sense when the extra reasoning depth changes the outcome: architecture, difficult debugging, exhaustive research, high-stakes review or long-running agent work.

Why Carbon matters for AI agents

The biggest story is not another benchmark score. Carbon arrives while the AI industry is rapidly moving from chat interfaces to agents that can act across tools for long periods. Recent industry reporting shows agentic traffic and production agent deployments accelerating quickly, which makes reliability over dozens or hundreds of steps more important than isolated question-answer accuracy.

Carbon is built for that environment. Google says it can run extended verification loops, coordinate agent roles and keep working through dead ends. In Google Antigravity, the new /swarm preview can split one complex request into a coordinated team of agents, with Carbon taking roles such as architectural planning, coordination and auditing while lighter models handle parallel subtasks.

This model architecture strategy is likely more important than “one model does everything.” A production agent system can route routine work to cheaper models and reserve Carbon for the hardest checkpoints.

What is Google Antigravity /swarm?

/swarm is Google's preview multi-agent feature inside Antigravity. Instead of asking a single agent to perform every task sequentially, a project can be divided across isolated workspaces and specialized agent roles.

Google demonstrates Carbon coordinating agents for complex 3D reconstruction, full-stack experiences and engineering tasks. The useful concept for developers is broader: a lead reasoning model can plan, delegate and audit while other agents work in parallel.

Practical architecture:

Carbon as planner/auditor → lighter agents for implementation/research → Carbon verification → build/test loop → human approval.

This can reduce the need to spend Carbon-level compute on every intermediate operation while still using it where reasoning quality matters most.

Gemini 4 Carbon for coding and software engineering

Google positions Carbon for engineering tasks where dependencies span multiple packages or systems. Examples include large migrations, cross-subsystem root-cause investigations, architectural redesign and full-stack development with iterative build-and-verify loops.

The model is intended to inspect failures, revise code and check the rendered or executed result rather than stopping after generating source code. That workflow matters because real engineering quality comes from the loop: understand → edit → run → inspect → fix → verify.

Google's November 10 announcement says Carbon ranked #1 on CursorBench 4.0 and cites third-party engineering evaluations. Those benchmark results are useful signals, but they do not prove that Carbon will be best for every codebase. Your language, tests, repository size, tools, permissions and agent harness can change the result significantly.

When Carbon is likely worth using for code

  • A bug spans multiple services and the root cause is unclear.
  • A migration touches a large codebase with strict backward compatibility.
  • You need architectural analysis before implementation.
  • An agent must run tests repeatedly and recover from failures.
  • A complex UI or 3D application needs both code and visual verification.
  • The cost of a subtle regression is much higher than the extra inference cost.

When Argon or a faster model may be better

  • Simple CRUD code.
  • Small refactors with clear tests.
  • Basic documentation.
  • Routine data transformation.
  • Short Q&A where long reasoning adds latency but little value.

Gemini 4 Carbon cybersecurity performance

Cybersecurity is where Google is making its strongest Carbon claims. At the initial October announcement, Google reported Carbon at 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro for vulnerability finding, 54.8% on ExploitGym and 71.0% on CWE-bench. Google's later availability post says its internal Project Fortify evaluation reached 100% recall for scanning and 95% precision in triage.

These numbers should be read as benchmark results, not guarantees for live networks. Security work depends on the target, tools, sandbox, permissions, attack surface and validation process.

Google also emphasizes safeguards because stronger defensive reasoning can create misuse risk. Its current launch post reports that Carbon blocked 99% of attack trajectories in a simulated adaptive red-team setup and reduced the modeled per-attempt breach rate versus Argon. Google also reports a 0% success rate on the Gray Swan indirect prompt-injection benchmark for the tested setup.

Security note: Use frontier security models only inside authorized environments. A benchmark score does not replace code review, sandboxing, access controls, vulnerability validation or responsible disclosure.

Carbon for documents, spreadsheets and multimodal knowledge work

Carbon is also designed for work that combines text with visually structured information: tables, charts, blueprints, schematics, PDFs and images. Google and Artificial Analysis introduced the Multimodal Knowledge Agent (MKA) benchmark alongside the public release to measure this kind of file-heavy agent work.

Google reports Carbon ranked second in quality in that benchmark while completing tasks at more than six times lower average cost than the highest-scoring model in the comparison. The benchmark uses 200 private evaluation tasks and a shared agent harness, with public sample tasks available for inspection.

The broader lesson is useful even if you never use Carbon: evaluating an AI agent on pure text questions is not enough when your real workflow depends on reading a complex PDF, preserving spreadsheet formulas or understanding a chart.

Gemini 4 Carbon for scientific research

Google describes Carbon as substantially stronger in physics, mathematics and life-science reasoning, especially when the model must combine raw data, literature, code and hypothesis testing.

One notable example in Google's release describes researchers using Carbon in a multi-agent workflow to search remote protein homologs across very large sequence and structure collections. Google says that across seven low-homolog protein families from the CASP17 blind test set, the workflow increased retrieved homologs by an average of 40 times. Google notes that broader benchmarking is still underway.

This is an important distinction: Carbon is not being positioned only as a chatbot for scientists. Google wants it to operate inside research loops where the model can inspect data, use tools and iterate.

Should you switch from Gemini 4 Argon to Carbon?

Not automatically. Carbon's value is highest when the problem has expensive uncertainty. If an easier model reliably finishes your work, using Carbon can add cost and latency without improving the business result.

Use a routing strategy:

  1. Classify the task. Is it routine, complex or high-risk?
  2. Start with the cheaper capable model.
  3. Escalate when uncertainty remains.
  4. Use Carbon for planning, audit or the hardest reasoning stage.
  5. Validate with tools and tests. A smarter model is not a replacement for verification.

This approach is especially useful for agent systems where hundreds of calls can occur in one workflow.

What Carbon means for the AI model race

Carbon shows how frontier competition is changing. Model makers are no longer optimizing only for a single benchmark or chat response. The new battleground is sustained work: how long a model can keep a plan coherent, whether it can use tools safely, whether it can recover from errors, how well it understands visual files, and what the completed task costs.

Recent industry trend reports also show agents becoming a normal production layer rather than an experimental feature. That makes end-to-end task completion and governance more important than raw token-level intelligence.

For developers, the consequence is clear: model evaluation should move from “Which model answered this prompt best?” to “Which model completed this workflow reliably at acceptable cost?”

Gemini 4 Carbon FAQ

Is Gemini 4 Carbon available now?

Yes. Google announced broader availability on November 10, 2026 for developers, enterprises, scientists and consumers through specific paid products and programs.

Is Gemini 4 Carbon free?

Google's broad public developer access is through the paid Gemini API tier, while consumer access is tied to Google AI Ultra in the products Google currently lists. Google's scientist program may provide eligible researchers access for a limited period.

How much does Gemini 4 Carbon cost?

Google's introductory public API price is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. Google states that after November 30, 2026 the listed public price becomes $4 input and $20 output per million tokens.

Is Carbon better than Argon?

Carbon is positioned as the stronger model for the hardest long-horizon tasks. Google still recommends Argon for the majority of software engineering and knowledge work, so “better” depends on whether the extra depth is useful for your task.

What is Carbon best at?

Google highlights complex coding, agentic workflows, cybersecurity defense, scientific reasoning, multimodal knowledge work, dense document analysis and difficult multi-step tasks.

Can Carbon run multiple AI agents?

Google is previewing a /swarm feature in Antigravity that can coordinate multiple agents. Carbon can be used for planning, coordination and auditing while other agents work in parallel.

Does Gemini 4 Carbon replace human cybersecurity experts?

No. Security outputs still need authorization, validation, scope controls, human judgment and responsible handling. Benchmarks demonstrate capabilities under defined conditions; they do not guarantee production accuracy.

Bottom line

Gemini 4 Carbon is not the model you need for every prompt. Its advantage is deeper, sustained work: complicated engineering, agent coordination, cybersecurity defense, scientific analysis and multimodal knowledge tasks where the cost of a shallow answer is high.

For everyday development and general knowledge work, Argon may remain the more efficient choice. For the hardest problems, Carbon gives Google's Gemini 4 family a dedicated high-reasoning tier.
